Section B: Short Questions

Q.1: Define the allotropy with examples?

Allotropy is the ability of an element to exist in different forms in the same physical state. These forms are called allotropes, and they have different properties.

Examples:

  • Carbon:
    • Diamond (hard and shiny)
    • Graphite (soft and used in pencils)
  • Oxygen:
    • O2 (Oxygen gas) (used for breathing)
    • O3 (Ozone) (protects from harmful sun rays)

Q.2: What is Effusion, explain with the examples?

Effusion is the movement of gas particles through a tiny hole without collisions between them.

Examples:

  • Gas leaking slowly from a small hole in a balloon.
  • The release of gas in a vacuum chamber.

Q.3: Define the following?

Boiling Point: The temperature at which a liquid changes into a gas at atmospheric pressure. (Example: Water boils at 100°C).

Melting Point: The temperature at which a solid changes into a liquid. (Example: Ice melts at 0°C).

Freezing Point: The temperature at which a liquid turns into a solid. (Example: Water freezes at 0°C).

Q.4: What is density, how the density of liquid is affected by temperature and pressure?

Density is the mass of a substance per unit volume. Usually expressed as Density (ρ) = Mass/Volume (m/V).

Temperature: As temperature increases, the density of a liquid decreases because the liquid expands.

Pressure: Liquids are nearly incompressible, so pressure has very little effect on their density. However, under extreme pressure, density may increase slightly.

Q.5: Explain plasma with daily life examples?

Plasma is the fourth state of matter, consisting of highly energized, ionized gas particles with free-moving electrons. It conducts electricity and responds to magnetic fields.

Daily Life Examples of Plasma:

  • Lightning – A natural plasma formed by ionized air during storms.
  • Neon Signs – Electrically charged gases inside tubes produce glowing plasma.
  • Flames – Fire contains partially ionized gas, making it a form of plasma.
  • The Sun & Stars – Made mostly of plasma due to extreme heat and pressure.
  • Plasma TVs – Use tiny plasma-filled cells to create images.

Q.6: Justify that atoms of Bose Einstein condensate are super unexcited and super cooled?

Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC) is a state of matter formed when atoms are cooled to extremely low temperatures, close to absolute zero (-273°C).

Super-cooled: At such low temperatures, atoms lose almost all their energy and move extremely slowly.

Super-unexcited: Since energy levels are minimal, atoms do not vibrate or react much, behaving as a single quantum entity. “This makes BEC unique, as particles act like one ‘super-atoms’ with shared properties.”

Q.7: How kinetic molecular theory differentiates states of matter?

The Kinetic Molecular Theory (KMT) explains the differences between states of matter based on particle movement and energy.

Differences According to KMT:

  • Solids: Particles are tightly packed, vibrate in place, and have low kinetic energy.
  • Liquids: Particles are loosely packed, move freely but stay close, with moderate kinetic energy.
  • Gases: Particles are far apart, move randomly at high speed, and have high kinetic energy.
  • Plasma: Particles are ionized and move extremely fast with very high kinetic energy.
“Thus, higher kinetic energy leads to more freedom of movement, changing the state of matter.”

Section C: Detailed Questions

Q.1: Discuss the property of evaporation in liquids? Which factors affect the evaporation process?

Evaporation is the process by which liquid molecules at the surface gain enough energy to escape into the gaseous state. It occurs below the boiling point and is a cooling process because it absorbs heat from the surroundings.

Factors Affecting Evaporation:
  • Temperature: Higher temperature increases molecular energy, speeding up evaporation.
  • Surface Area: A larger surface area allows more molecules to escape, increasing evaporation.
  • Humidity: Higher humidity reduces evaporation because the air already has more water vapor.
  • Wind Speed: Fast-moving air removes evaporated molecules, allowing more evaporation.
  • Nature of Liquid: Volatile liquids (like alcohol) evaporate faster than less volatile ones (like water).
  • Air Pressure: Lower air pressure increases evaporation, as molecules face less resistance.

This explains why clothes dry faster in hot, dry, and windy conditions.

Q.2: Describe Boyl's Law with example?

Boyle’s Law states that the pressure of a gas is inversely proportional to its volume when temperature and the amount of gas remain constant.

Mathematical Expression:

P ∝ 1/V, or P1V1 = P2V2

Examples:

  • Syringe: When you pull the plunger of a syringe, the volume inside increases, causing the pressure to decrease, which draws liquid into the syringe.
  • Scuba Diving: As a diver goes deeper, water pressure increases, compressing the air in the diving tank.

Q.3: Differentiate between Amorphous and Crystalline Solids?

Difference between Amorphous and Crystalline Solids:

Property Crystalline Solids Amorphous Solids
Arrangement of Particles Regular and ordered pattern Random and disordered pattern
Shape Definite geometric shape No definite shape
Melting Point Sharp melting point No fixed melting point (softens over a range of temperatures)
Cleavage Property Breaks into clean, flat surface Breaks into irregular, curved pieces
Examples Salt, Diamond, Quartz Glass, Rubber, Plastic

Q.4: Define and explain Charles' Law of gases?

Charles’ Law states that the volume of a gas is directly proportional to its temperature (in Kelvin) when pressure and the amount of gas remain constant.

Mathematical Expression:

V ∝ T

Explanation:

  • When the temperature of a gas increases, its volume expands because gas molecules move faster.
  • When the temperature decreases, the volume contracts as molecules slow down and occupy less space.

Examples:

  • Hot Air Balloon: When air inside a balloon is heated, it expands, making the balloon rise.
  • Deflating Football in cold weather: A football left outside in the cold shrinks because lower temperature reduces air volume inside.

Q.5: Describe the process of diffusion in liquids? State the factors which influence it?

Diffusion is the process by which particles of one liquid spread into another without external force due to random molecular motion. It occurs slowly in liquids compared to gases because liquid molecules are closer together and move less freely.

Example of Diffusion in Liquids:

  • Ink in Water: When a drop of ink is added to water, it gradually spreads throughout the water without stirring.

Factors Affecting Diffusion in Liquids:

  1. Temperature:Higher temperature increases molecular movement, speeding up diffusion.
  2. Density of Liquid: (denser) liquids slow down diffusion.
  3. Viscosity:: Thick (viscous) liquids like honey slow diffusion, while thin liquids like water allow faster diffusion.
  4. Size of Particles: Smaller molecules diffuse faster than larger ones.

• Thus, diffusion in liquids depends upon temperature, density, viscosity, and particle size.
